# RESEARCH-RAILWAY-ALIGNMENT-HISTORY-001

## 250 Years of Railway Alignment Knowledge, ca. 1775–2026

Status: Research synthesis; not Kernel canon.

This package contains a source-critical longitudinal study of how railway
alignment knowledge developed from wagonway and surveying practice into
constructive geometry, dynamics, measurement, computation, optimization, and
digital infrastructure models.

## Central finding

The history is not a smooth accumulation of formulas. It is a sequence of
changes in the object being designed:

1. a marked route through terrain;
2. a chain of tangents and circular arcs;
3. a speed-dependent spatial path with transition curvature, cant, and vertical
   profile;
4. a maintained and measured track whose actual geometry differs from design;
5. a referenced network object represented in several coordinate, topology,
   asset, and exchange systems;
6. an evaluated, provenance-bearing knowledge object.

Railway specificity lies especially in the coupling of guided wheel–rail
motion, cant, cant deficiency, transition length, vehicle dynamics, operational
speed, switches, and network continuity. Surveying, geodesy, differential
geometry, mechanics, road design, numerical analysis, and information modelling
supplied methods, but did not by themselves define the railway engineering
problem.

## Evidential caution

Dates refer to the earliest source verified in this mission, not necessarily to
first invention or first field use. “First”, “introduced”, and priority claims
are avoided unless a primary source or specialist historical study supports
them. Several historical digitizations expose later editions; edition date and
original appearance are distinguished where known.
