\chapter{Dynamics and Railway State Interpretation}

Geometric alignment alone is not sufficient to describe railway
behaviour. Operational behaviour depends on coupled evolution of
curvature, cant, and speed. This chapter gives the canonical dynamic
interpretation of railway state quantities that are derived from the
state layers established in this part.

% ============================================================
\section{Kinematic Basis}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Velocity]
Let \(v>0\) denote the vehicle velocity along the alignment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Curvature-induced acceleration]
For curvature \(\kappa(s)\), the lateral acceleration induced by planar
motion is
\[
a_\kappa(s) = v^2 \kappa(s).
\]
\end{definition}

This is the classical centripetal contribution and forms the basic link
between intrinsic geometry and dynamics.

% ============================================================
\section{Cant and Effective Lateral Acceleration}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Cant angle]
Let \(\phi(s)\) denote the local roll angle induced by cant.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Gravity component]
The gravitational acceleration projected onto the lateral direction is
\[
a_g(s) = g\sin\phi(s),
\]
where \(g\) denotes gravitational acceleration.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Effective lateral acceleration]
The effective lateral acceleration acting on the vehicle is
\[
a_{\mathrm{eff}}(s)
=
v^2\kappa(s) - g\sin\phi(s).
\]
\end{definition}

This expression captures the canonical coupling between curvature and
cant in railway engineering and vehicle dynamics
\cite{Klein1998Trassierung,EN13803_2017,BrustadDalmo2020Review,Iwnicki2006RailVehicleDynamics,Wickens2003Fundamentals}.

% ============================================================
\section{Equilibrium and Cant Deficiency}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Equilibrium cant]
A balanced state satisfies
\[
v^2\kappa(s) = g\sin\phi(s).
\]
\end{definition}

Under this condition,
\[
a_{\mathrm{eff}}(s)=0.
\]

\begin{definition}[Cant deficiency]
Cant deficiency is defined as
\[
\Delta(s)
=
v^2\kappa(s) - g\sin\phi(s).
\]
\end{definition}

Positive values indicate insufficient cant, negative values indicate
excess cant. Cant deficiency is a central operational quantity for
comfort and safety interpretation
\cite{EN13803_2017,BrustadDalmo2020Review,MagalhaesAmbrosioPombo2016Compatibility}.

% ============================================================
\section{Dynamic Smoothness and Jerk}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Jerk]
Jerk is the time derivative of effective lateral acceleration:
\[
j(s)=\frac{\dd}{\dd t}a_{\mathrm{eff}}(s).
\]
\end{definition}

Using \(\dd t = \frac{1}{v}\,\dd s\), one obtains
\[
j(s)=v\frac{\dd}{\dd s}a_{\mathrm{eff}}(s).
\]

\begin{proposition}
The jerk relation is
\[
j(s)=v^3\kappa'(s)-gv\cos\phi(s)\,\phi'(s).
\]
\end{proposition}

This relation shows that both curvature variation and cant variation
contribute to runtime smoothness and comfort behaviour
\cite{Mieloszyk2012Transition,Koc2017SmoothedEnds,Koc2019Operational,KnotheGrassie1993HighFrequencies}.

% ============================================================
\section{Arc-Length and Time}
% ============================================================

The alignment is parameterized by arc length \(s\), whereas physical
response is observed in time \(t\).

\begin{definition}[Arc-length/time relation]
Assuming constant speed \(v>0\),
\[
\frac{\dd s}{\dd t}=v.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
For any quantity \(f(s)\),
\[
\frac{\dd f}{\dd t}=v\frac{\dd f}{\dd s}.
\]
\end{proposition}

This relation provides the bridge between arc-length state evolution and
time-based operational interpretation.

% ============================================================
\section{State-Space Interpretation}
% ============================================================

A minimal geometric state formulation is
\[
(x(s),y(s),\theta(s),\phi(s)),
\]
with intrinsic evolution
\[
\frac{\dd x}{\dd s}=\cos\theta(s),
\qquad
\frac{\dd y}{\dd s}=\sin\theta(s),
\qquad
\frac{\dd\theta}{\dd s}=\kappa(s).
\]

Within this chapter, \(\kappa(s)\) and \(\phi(s)\) act as primary
inputs to derived dynamic quantities. This is consistent with reduced
vehicle-track interpretations where track state provides structured
runtime input to dynamics models
\cite{Iwnicki2006RailVehicleDynamics,Shabana2008RailroadVehicleDynamics}.

% ============================================================
\section{Coupled Interpretation and Optimization Relevance}
% ============================================================

Railway behaviour depends on the coupled variables
\[
(\kappa(s),\phi(s)).
\]
A purely geometric interpretation based on \(\kappa(s)\) alone is
insufficient for physical behaviour. The derived quantities in this
chapter therefore provide canonical objective and constraint terms for
alignment optimization, for example integrals of
\(a_{\mathrm{eff}}^2\), \(j^2\), and cant-deficiency bounds
\cite{Kufver1997MathematicalDescription,Kufver2000RailwayAlignment,UIC519}.

% ============================================================
\section{Connection to AIM}
% ============================================================

\begin{summary}
Dynamics in AIM is downstream of railway state evaluation. Curvature and
cant are persistently represented, while effective acceleration,
deficiency, and jerk are derived runtime quantities.

This preserves the layer separation between intrinsic geometry,
realization, railway runtime state, and operational interpretation while
providing the dynamic basis for admissibility and optimization.
\end{summary}
