\chapter{From Planar Pose to Spatial Railway State}
\label{chap:pose3-and-cant}

At the selected transition position, pose2 supplies planar location and
heading. A railway cross-section still cannot be placed: the Alignment's
horizontal curvature law contains neither elevation nor rail-plane rotation.
Spatial interpretation therefore needs additional, independently qualified
longitudinal laws.

\section{Additional Laws, Not Additional Identity}

Let \(h(s)\) denote the elevation law in the chosen metric realization.
Where supported, \(h'(s)\) supplies gradient and \(h''(s)\) contributes
vertical-curvature information under the adopted profile convention. Let
\(u(s)\) denote cant as a rail-height difference:

\begin{definition}[Cant height difference]
\label{def:cant-height-difference}
\[
u\colon D_u\rightarrow \mathbb R
\]
is a longitudinal cant law with its own domain \(D_u\), units, partition,
provenance, and applicability.
\end{definition}

Cant is not horizontal curvature. The laws \(\kappa(s)\), \(h(s)\), and
\(u(s)\) may have different breakpoints and sources. Their common parameter
allows synchronized evaluation; it does not make them one property or give
one law authority over another.

\begin{definition}[Cant reference convention]
\label{def:cant-reference-convention}
A cant convention states the sign, rotation axis, gauge interpretation,
reference line or rail, and any induced reference-line displacement.
\end{definition}

For the explanatory convention used here only, \(u\) is measured across a
stated gauge \(g_{\mathrm{gauge}}>0\), the centerline remains the reference,
and positive roll follows the displayed right-handed frame. Then
\begin{equation}
\phi(s)=
\arctan\!\left(\frac{u(s)}{g_{\mathrm{gauge}}}\right).
\label{eq:derived-roll-angle}
\end{equation}
This equation converts one qualified representation of cant into a roll
angle. It is not a universal cant convention and says nothing about which
cant law should be designed.

\section{The Moving Railway Frame}

\begin{definition}[Uncanted geometric frame]
\label{def:uncanted-frame}
At \(s\), let
\[
F_0(s)=\bigl(\mathbf t(s),\mathbf l_0(s),\mathbf z_0(s)\bigr)
\]
be an oriented orthonormal frame: \(\mathbf t\) is longitudinal, while
\(\mathbf l_0\) and \(\mathbf z_0\) are the lateral and vertical directions
supplied by a stated realization and transport convention.
\end{definition}

Horizontal curvature controls change of heading. Vertical geometry controls
the slope of \(\mathbf t\). Metric realization supplies the spatial reference
and vertical interpretation. A continuity rule transports the cross-section
through straights and low-curvature regions where a pure Frenet construction
would be unstable.

\begin{definition}[Railway frame]
\label{def:railway-frame}
The cant-aware track frame is
\[
F_{\mathrm{rail}}(s)=
\bigl(\mathbf t(s),\mathbf l_\phi(s),\mathbf z_\phi(s)\bigr),
\qquad
(\mathbf l_\phi,\mathbf z_\phi)
=R_{\mathbf t,\phi(s)}(\mathbf l_0,\mathbf z_0).
\]
\end{definition}

This local right-handed construction is selected to make dependencies
visible; it does not establish a universal AIM frame convention. Source
conventions must be transformed explicitly before signs or rail positions are
compared.

\begin{definition}[Vehicle frame]
\label{def:vehicle-frame}
\(F_{\mathrm{veh}}(s)\) denotes an optional vehicle-dependent frame produced
from the track frame together with a stated vehicle and suspension model.
\end{definition}

A vehicle frame cannot be inferred from Alignment geometry alone.

\section{What pose3 Adds}

\begin{definition}[Runtime pose3]
\label{def:runtime-pose3}
For this Thesis-level explanation, the spatial railway pose at \(s\) is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{pose3}(s)=
\bigl(\Gamma(s),F_{\mathrm{rail}}(s),
h'(s),h''(s),u(s)\bigr),
\label{eq:runtime-pose3}
\end{equation}
with unavailable or inapplicable profile terms omitted and all quantities
qualified by their realization and convention.
\end{definition}

\(\Gamma(s)\) is the realized spatial reference trajectory assembled from
pose2, profile, metric interpretation, and coordinate services. The frame
operator \(\mathcal F\) of \cref{def:frame-operator} expresses the typed
evaluation. Pose3 adds the quantities needed to orient a railway
cross-section; it is not the complete physical asset and does not include
materials, condition, topology, vehicle behaviour, or engineering authority.

\begin{principle}[pose3 reconstruction principle]
\label{prop:pose3-reconstruction}
Given \(\kappa(s)\), \(h(s)\), and \(u(s)\), together with a starting pose,
metric realization, gauge, orientation and frame conventions, the applicable
pose3 fields can be evaluated. The result preserves its Alignment reference
and the provenance of every additional input.
\end{principle}

The evaluability follows only after metric, frame, transport, gauge, and
convention operators have been chosen; the preservation of Alignment
reference and provenance is a contract on those operators, not a
mathematical consequence of \(\kappa\), \(h\), and \(u\).

\section{Target, Realization, Physical State, and Observation}

The intended Alignment and its design laws produce a \emph{target pose}.
A Metric Realization places that target in a metric space. Construction
produces a physical asset whose state varies with time. Surveying produces
time-stamped observations or observed-state estimates in represented
coordinates. These are related, not interchangeable.

A CAD curve may represent the target trajectory. A survey polyline may
represent observations of the rails. A Soll--Ist comparison must qualify both
sides by object reference, time, realization, convention, provenance, and
uncertainty before a deviation can be interpreted. Coordinate subtraction
alone cannot decide which state is correct or acceptable.

\section{Cant and Curvature Meet Downstream}

Curvature and cant remain separate laws even when an operational calculation
uses both. The relations in
\cref{eq:effective-lateral-acceleration,eq:cant-equilibrium,eq:jerk-relation}
are simplified, assumption-dependent interpretations introduced for the
subsequent dynamics discussion. They are not authoritative design coupling
rules. Applicable standards, vehicle models, speed profiles, and decisions
remain separate inputs.

\begin{summary}
Pose3 is a qualified spatial state derived from the identified Alignment plus
profile, cant, metric realization, and frame convention. Cant rotates the
railway frame but neither changes the identity of \(\kappa(s)\) nor supplies
engineering authority.
\end{summary}

\begin{proposition}[Track state principle]
Pose3 describes a qualified railway pose. Vehicle states require additional
vehicle-space operators and models.
\end{proposition}
