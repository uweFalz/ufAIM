\chapter{Schwerpunkt-Trassierung as a Qualified Model Idea}

\section*{The idea in one page}

The historical attraction of \emph{Schwerpunkt-Trassierung} is easy to state.
A track centreline is not the path of every dynamically relevant point on a
vehicle. If an Alignment edit changes curvature, the motion predicted for a
chosen vehicle reference point can change as well. Berlinish and axtranNew
project thinking used this insight to look beyond isolated curve formulae;
engineering and patent literature likewise relates alignment to vehicle
centre-of-mass motion \cite{Hasslinger2002Schwerpunkt}.

That origin is strong and useful, but it is not validation. The word
\emph{Schwerpunkt} can refer to several different things:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.29\linewidth}p{0.28\linewidth}p{0.34\linewidth}}
\toprule
Use & Required qualification & Status here\\
\midrule
physical centre of mass & particular vehicle and loading & physical property\\
model reference point \(P_M\) & model \(M\), frame and parameters & predicted model quantity\\
Berlinish/axtranNew idea & source and historical purpose & project provenance\\
optimization aggregate & variables, objective and scaling & problem-specific construction\\
``centre'' as emphasis & no mathematical object & metaphor only\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This chapter investigates the second and fourth meanings. It neither creates a
canonical \emph{Schwerpunkt State} nor changes Alignment identity. The reader
arrives here from the contract established in
\cref{chap:optimization-in-state-space}:
\[
\text{model}\to\text{objective}\to\text{candidate}
\to\text{evaluation}\to\text{Engineering Decision}.
\]

\section{Historical proposition, present boundary}

The original proposition was that transition design might be judged through a
representative vehicle-related trajectory rather than through track geometry
alone. Its originality lies in connecting constructive curvature, cant and
vehicle-oriented consequence within one computational question. It remains a
research and modelling proposition: no active Kernel authority establishes a
Schwerpunkt principle, one universal reference point, one response model or one
objective.

The physically relevant centre of mass belongs to a particular loaded vehicle
and changes with loading. A model reference point \(P_M\) belongs instead to an
identified model \(M\). It may coincide with an assumed centre of mass only
under explicit rigid-body, loading and frame assumptions. A representative
trajectory or weighted optimization aggregate need not coincide with either.

\section{A deliberately reduced reference-point model}

Let \(\gamma(s)\) be a planar realization of the Alignment, with tangent
\(t(s)\), normal \(n(s)\), heading \(\theta(s)\) and curvature \(\kappa(s)\):
\[
t=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\qquad
n=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\qquad n'=-\kappa t.
\]
Choose the illustrative model
\[
M_{\mathrm{off}}=(P_M,d,F_{\mathrm{track}},
\text{rigid offset},\text{planar realization}),
\]
where \(d\) is a fixed signed offset. Its represented reference-point path is
\[
\gamma_{P_M}(s)=\gamma(s)+d\,n(s).
\]

This is a geometric output of \(M_{\mathrm{off}}\), not a measured vehicle
trajectory and not automatically a physical centre-of-mass path. Cant,
suspension, inertia, wheel--rail contact, load transfer, track flexibility and
irregularities are omitted.

Differentiation with respect to station gives
\[
\gamma_{P_M}'=(1-d\kappa)t,
\]
\[
\gamma_{P_M}''
=-d\kappa't+(1-d\kappa)\kappa n.
\]
These are station derivatives. They become temporal kinematics only after a
qualified traversal \(s(t)\) is supplied:
\[
\dot\gamma_{P_M}=v\,\gamma_{P_M}',\qquad
\ddot\gamma_{P_M}=\dot v\,\gamma_{P_M}'+v^2\gamma_{P_M}''.
\]
Even then, they describe the prescribed point path of the reduced model, not a
vehicle-body response solved from mechanics.

\section{From model output to an optimization problem}

An illustrative investigation can ask whether changing a transition reduces
variation of this model path. It must first declare the problem:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.27\linewidth}p{0.63\linewidth}}
\toprule
Contract field & Declaration in the example\\
\midrule
problem identity & reference-point path sensitivity for one transition\\
model & \(M_{\mathrm{off}}\) with stated \(d\) and planar track frame\\
decision variables & selected transition parameters \(x=(L,\alpha)\)\\
fixed inputs & endpoint states, family, realization, \(d\), traversal test\\
derived quantities & \(\kappa,\gamma,\gamma_{P_M}\) and their derivatives\\
constraints & continuity, parameter bounds and declared engineering bounds\\
unresolved freedoms & cant and vehicle response unless explicitly added\\
provenance & Berlinish/axtranNew-inspired modelling experiment\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

One possible geometric sensitivity objective is
\[
J_P(x)=\int_0^{L(x)}
\left\|\gamma_{P_M}''(s;x)\right\|^2\,\dd s .
\]
Its source is the stated experiment; it measures a station-domain path
property. It is not self-evidently a comfort, safety, wear, cost or energy
objective.

If the reduced balance residual is also examined, a separate objective may be
\[
J_r(x)=\int_0^{T(x)}
r_{\mathrm{bal}}(t;x)^2\,\dd t,
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{bal}}=v^2\kappa-g\sin\phi,
\]
under the assumptions of \cref{chap:dynamic-balance}. The two objectives have
different units and meanings. A combined objective
\[
J=w_PJ_P/J_{P,0}+w_rJ_r/J_{r,0}
\]
is defined only when a responsible source supplies the scales, weights,
purpose and applicability. Neither the model nor the solver owns those
choices.

\section{Degrees of freedom and candidate status}

In the bounded example, \(L\) and \(\alpha\) are free within declared bounds.
Endpoint states and the chosen family are fixed; coefficients and terminal
pose are derived; continuity is constrained. Cant, traversal, \(d\), vehicle
parameters or realization data may be fixed, made variable, or left
unresolved, but their role must be recorded. They do not become variables
merely because the word \emph{Schwerpunkt} is used.

A solver result is therefore
\[
C_{\mathrm{SP}}=
(\mathcal P.id,M_{\mathrm{off}}.id,x^\star,J,
\text{residuals},\text{convergence},\text{provenance}),
\]
a calculation candidate. It remains subject to applicability evaluation
against qualified evidence, rules, lifecycle and purpose. Selection or
rejection requires an Engineering Decision with authority.

\[
\boxed{\text{calculated}
\neq\text{applicable}
\neq\text{accepted}
\neq\text{authoritative}}
\]

The current continuity solver supports only the bounded continuity claim
already stated in \cref{chap:optimization-in-state-space}. This chapter does
not claim current AXTRAN vehicle, cant, comfort, network or automatic
engineering-selection optimization.

\section{Scientific potential without canonization}

The model idea survives with qualification. More capable work may replace
\(M_{\mathrm{off}}\) with an identified rigid-body or multibody model, calibrate
parameters from qualified evidence, propagate observation and model-form
uncertainty, or compare several vehicle reference points. Each extension must
state its subject, model, frame, inputs, validity and purpose.

The historical metaphor remains productive because it asks a better downstream
question: what consequence does an Alignment construction produce for a
specified vehicle-related model? The answer remains model-specific evidence.
It does not redefine Alignment, establish universal validity or decide what
engineering shall accept.
