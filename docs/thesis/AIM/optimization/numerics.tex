\chapter{Numerical Reconstruction for a Reviewable Candidate}

\section{Why a curvature edit needs numerics}

In the running example, the free variables change selected coefficients of an
intrinsic curvature law. Yet the fixed endpoint, direction, and survey
observations are spatial quantities. Each trial vector \(x\) must therefore be
reconstructed before the problem can say how well it fits.

Let
\[
\kappa_x:[0,L]\rightarrow\R
\]
be the candidate curvature, and let the fixed start pose be
\((\gamma_0,\theta_0)\). The model \(M\) produces
\[
\theta_x'(s)=\kappa_x(s),\qquad
\gamma_x'(s)=(\cos\theta_x(s),\sin\theta_x(s)).
\]
This reconstruction produces a representation used for evaluation. It neither
changes Alignment identity nor turns the represented curve into an accepted
engineering state.

\section{From a continuous model to computable samples}

\paragraph{Reader bridge.}
The equations state the dependency exactly, but a solver must evaluate it
finitely and repeatedly. Discretization is the declared approximation that
makes this possible.

Choose
\[
0=s_0<s_1<\dots<s_N=L,\qquad \Delta s_i=s_{i+1}-s_i .
\]
For example, a midpoint step gives
\[
\theta_{i+\frac12}=\theta_i+
 \frac12\kappa_x(s_i)\Delta s_i,
\]
\[
\gamma_{i+1}=\gamma_i+\Delta s_i
(\cos\theta_{i+\frac12},\sin\theta_{i+\frac12}),\qquad
\theta_{i+1}=\theta_i+\kappa_x(s_i)\Delta s_i.
\]
Other integration schemes may be appropriate. The method, tolerance, and
sampling rule are part of \(M\); they are not invisible implementation detail.

\section{Residuals answer declared questions}

For the fixed target end pose \((\bar\gamma_L,\bar\theta_L)\), a model may
produce the endpoint residual
\[
r_{\mathrm{end}}(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\gamma_x(L)-\bar\gamma_L\\
\operatorname{wrap}(\theta_x(L)-\bar\theta_L)
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For survey observations \(y_j\), an observation model \(h_j\) may produce
\[
r_{\mathrm{obs},j}(x)=
h_j(\gamma_x)-y_j.
\]
These residuals answer different questions and can have different units,
uncertainties, and responsibilities. A constraint residual records
feasibility; an observation residual records discrepancy under an observation
model. They must not be silently pooled.

If residual families enter one least-squares objective, scaling must be
declared, for example
\[
J(x)=
\frac12\|W_{\mathrm{end}}r_{\mathrm{end}}(x)\|_2^2+
\frac12\sum_j
\|W_{\mathrm{obs},j}r_{\mathrm{obs},j}(x)\|_2^2+
J_{\mathrm{reg}}(x).
\]
The matrices \(W\) make units and relative influence explicit; their values
come from the stated purpose, uncertainty model, or responsible engineering
choice, not from the numerical method.

\section{Accuracy belongs to the result evidence}

\paragraph{Reader bridge.}
A candidate may appear feasible because of a coarse grid rather than because
the underlying continuous model satisfies the constraint. Numerical error
must therefore travel with the result.

Relevant contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
\item integration and discretization error;
\item interpolation or quadrature error in observation residuals;
\item floating-point and linear-solve error;
\item sensitivity of reconstructed position to curvature perturbations;
\item model-form and observation uncertainty, which numerical refinement
      cannot remove.
\end{itemize}

A defensible run records the method and mesh, tolerances, refinement checks,
and residuals recomputed on an independent or finer evaluation grid. A
floating origin or local metric frame can reduce numerical loss for large
coordinate values, but such a representation change does not alter the
intrinsic Alignment.

\section{Why derivatives and sparsity appear next}

Repeated reconstruction makes the dependence
\[
x\longmapsto\gamma_x\longmapsto r(x)\longmapsto J(x)
\]
computationally visible. Derivatives quantify how a permitted parameter
change affects the residuals. Sparsity records that a local curvature
parameter usually affects only structured portions of the assembled problem.
Both are responses to cost and traceability needs; neither selects the
engineering objective.

The numerical chapter has therefore supplied the evaluator required by the
optimization problem:
\[
(x;M,q)\longmapsto
\bigl(\widehat\gamma_x,r(x),J(x),\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}\bigr).
\]
Its output is evidence about a trial, not a decision about the Alignment.

\begin{summary}
Numerical reconstruction makes a declared curvature edit evaluable. Its
discretization, scaling, tolerances, and error estimates remain visible so
that an apparently good solver result can be reviewed. The next chapter can
now ask how to search the freedoms efficiently without changing who owns the
problem or the decision.
\end{summary}
