\chapter{Qualified Realization-Aware Optimization}
\index{Optimization!realization-aware}

\section*{Abstract for the general reader}

An analytically smooth design is not guaranteed to be constructed exactly as
drawn. Machines, materials, construction choices, maintenance and uncertainty
can make the physical asset differ from design intent. Realization-aware
optimization asks a useful question: if a declared realization model predicts
such differences, should candidate design parameters be chosen with those
predicted consequences in view?

The answer is conditional. A coordinate transformation is not construction.
An observation is not the physical asset. A model prediction is not an
observation, and an optimum is not an Engineering Decision. This chapter
therefore develops a candidate formulation, not a universal or continuously
operating AIM capability.

\section{Five objects that must remain separate}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.25\linewidth}p{0.31\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}}
\toprule
Object & Role & Not interchangeable with\\
\midrule
Metric Realization & makes intrinsic construction spatially measurable & physical construction\\
Physical Realization & relation from intent and process to an asset & coordinate transformation\\
Physical State & asset condition at a stated time & observation\\
Observation & source- and uncertainty-qualified evidence & physical truth\\
Representation & carries coordinates or records & identity, reality or authority\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Let \(S_{\mathrm{int}}(x)\) be intended state produced by design variables
\(x\). A qualified physical-realization model is written
\[
\widehat S_{\mathrm{phys}}
\in
\mathcal R_M\!\left(
S_{\mathrm{int}}(x),q,\xi
\right),
\]
where \(M\) identifies the model, \(q\) contains fixed process and asset
inputs, and \(\xi\) represents declared variability or uncertainty.
\(\mathcal R_M\) may be deterministic, stochastic or set-valued. The notation
does not establish that construction itself is an executable AIM operator.

An observation model remains separate:
\[
y^{\mathrm{obs}}
=
\mathcal O_N(S_{\mathrm{phys}},\eta),
\]
with observation method \(N\) and uncertainty \(\eta\). Coordinate conversion
may be required to compare quantities in one frame, but it changes
representation rather than physical state.

\section{Problem contract before the minimum}

A realization-aware optimization problem is defined only after recording:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.26\linewidth}p{0.64\linewidth}}
\toprule
Contract field & Required declaration\\
\midrule
problem identity and purpose & engineering question and lifecycle use\\
realization relation & \(M\), inputs, output, frame, validity and status\\
decision variables & known, fixed, free, bounded and unresolved quantities\\
fixed inputs & intended endpoints, context, process assumptions and evidence\\
feasible set \(\mathcal F\) & geometric, continuity, rule and model-applicability constraints\\
objective \(J\) & source, units, scales, aggregation and purpose\\
uncertainty boundary & variability represented and effects omitted\\
status and provenance & implementation-backed, Research-supported or illustrative\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Only then may a candidate problem be written:
\[
\min_{x\in\mathcal F}
J\!\left(
x,\widehat S_{\mathrm{phys}},
\mathcal D_K(\widehat S_{\mathrm{phys}},s(t))
\right),
\]
where \(K\) is an identified response or comparison model when one is needed.
If \(\mathcal R_M\) is uncertain, the problem must state whether \(J\) uses an
expectation, scenarios, a bound, a risk measure or another declared
construction. No uncertainty treatment is implied by the word
\emph{realization-aware}.

\section{Curvature and cant: coupled question, separate ownership}

Curvature \(\kappa(s)\) belongs to intrinsic horizontal construction. Cant
\(u(s)\), or its angle \(\phi(s)\), is a separate longitudinal law with its
own provenance and responsibility. A model may couple their consequences, for
example through the reduced residual
\[
r_{\mathrm{bal}}=v^2\kappa-g\sin\phi,
\]
without making the two laws one quantity.

The decision vector may therefore be any explicitly selected partition:
\[
x=(x_\kappa,x_u,x_p).
\]
In one problem, \(x_\kappa\) may be free while cant is fixed. In another,
curvature is fixed and cant is investigated. Both may be free only when the
purpose, constraints and responsible objective source support joint
investigation. There is no universal AIM rule requiring joint optimization.

\section{Objectives, units and scaling}

Possible objective components include curvature variation, cant variation,
the reduced balance residual, response-model output, cost or realization
sensitivity. They are distinct objectives. For example,
\[
J_\kappa=\int_0^L(\kappa')^2\,\dd s,
\qquad
J_r=\int_0^T r_{\mathrm{bal}}^2\,\dd t
\]
have different domains, units and meanings. \(J_r\) is not comfort or vehicle
response without an applicable model and criterion.

A combined objective
\[
J=\sum_i w_i\,J_i/J_{i,0}
\]
is meaningful only when the reference scales \(J_{i,0}\), weights \(w_i\),
their source and their purpose are recorded. The solver supplies none of
these engineering choices.

\section{Constraints and applicability}

Candidate constraints may describe machine limits, continuity, permitted
parameter ranges, process envelopes or applicable rules. Each constraint
requires a source, units, validity and lifecycle scope. Measurement resolution
is an observation qualification, not automatically a construction constraint.
Tamping behaviour, ballast response or correction kernels enter only through
an identified and supported realization model.

The feasible set \(\mathcal F\) establishes mathematical feasibility under
that declaration. It does not establish constructability, operational
acceptability or authority outside the model's applicability.

\section{Uncertainty and calibration boundary}

The minimum useful separation is:
\begin{itemize}
\item process and construction variability in \(\xi\);
\item observation uncertainty in \(\eta\);
\item operational variability in \(s(t)\) and other use inputs;
\item numerical residual from the solver;
\item parameter uncertainty inside \(M\) or \(K\);
\item model-form uncertainty about whether \(M\) or \(K\) is adequate.
\end{itemize}

Calibration evidence may support parameters for a stated asset, process and
domain. Agreement with one observation does not prove a universal
realization law. An unexplained target--observation deviation does not identify
construction as its cause.

\section{Inverse and frequency interpretations}

The expression
\[
\mathcal R_M(S_{\mathrm{int}}(x),q,\xi)
\approx S_{\mathrm{desired}}
\]
defines an inverse problem only after the comparison quantity, norm,
uncertainty treatment and applicability are declared. A solution is one
candidate pre-image under \(M\), not proof of what the physical process will
produce.

Likewise, describing \(\mathcal R_M\) as a low-pass filter is a possible
linearized or empirical model, not a property of Physical Realization in
general. Frequency penalties are defensible only when evidence supports that
model over the stated spatial or temporal range.

\section{Repeated optimization is a workflow hypothesis}
\index{Optimization!runtime}

Construction, maintenance or observation feedback may motivate repeated
problem formulation and recalculation:
\[
\text{new evidence}
\to\text{qualified model update}
\to\text{new candidate}
\to\text{evaluation}.
\]
That sequence does not imply a continuously operating optimizer, automatic
control or current AXTRAN runtime functionality. Such a workflow requires
identified triggers, data qualification, model versioning, convergence
handling, authority and implementation evidence. In this Thesis it remains a
possible workflow and Research direction.

\section{Candidate, evaluation and decision}

A result shall preserve at least
\[
C_R=
(\mathcal P.id,M.id,K.id,x^\star,\mathcal F,J,
\text{residuals},\text{convergence},\text{scenarios},\text{provenance}).
\]
It is a non-authoritative calculation candidate. Later evidence may make it
inapplicable or stale. Purpose-qualified evaluation may compare it with rules
and evidence; an Engineering Decision may then select or reject it under
documented authority. The dependency is the one shown in
\cref{fig:model-candidate-decision}.

\[
\boxed{\text{calculated}
\neq\text{applicable}
\neq\text{accepted}
\neq\text{authoritative}}
\]

\section{Vehicle, Earth and implementation boundaries}

Where a purpose requires vehicle-related quantities, an identified response or
clearance model supplies them. The qualified Schwerpunkt example is referenced
without redefining it: physical centre of mass, model reference point,
envelope and clearance remain different subjects.

Ellipsoidal Metric Realization may matter at large scale or high precision,
but it does not make physical construction an ellipsoid operator. Coordinate
and surface models qualify where quantities are evaluated; they do not create
an optimization objective.

The formulations in this chapter are scientifically usable problem contracts
and candidates. They are not evidence that current AIM, AXTRAN, transitionDB
or TransEd implements general realization-aware, vehicle, construction or
runtime optimization.

The potential remains substantial: design candidates can be tested against
explicit realization hypotheses instead of assuming perfect construction.
The scientific gain comes from making the hypothesis, uncertainty and
responsibility inspectable, not from calling the result universally
realization-aware.
