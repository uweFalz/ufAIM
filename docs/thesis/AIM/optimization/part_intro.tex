\section*{Reader abstract}

Optimization helps when an engineering problem admits more than one possible
change. It searches declared freedoms for candidates that satisfy declared
constraints and compare well under a declared objective. It cannot supply the
purpose of that objective, establish the applicability of its inputs, accept a
candidate, or make an Engineering Decision.

This part follows one example. A survey polyline and a CAD curve disagree in a
transition region. The Alignment identity is fixed. The engineer permits a
bounded edit of selected intrinsic curvature parameters, while the endpoints,
end directions, project context, and source observations remain fixed. The
question is not ``Which curve is true?'' but:
\begin{quote}
Which curvature edit is a reviewable candidate for this stated purpose, under
these constraints, assumptions, and observations?
\end{quote}

\section*{The contract before the calculation}

An optimization problem is meaningful only after its responsibilities have
been declared. For the running example, let
\[
\mathcal P=(I,P,M,x,q,\mathcal F,J,\Sigma),
\]
where \(I\) identifies the Engineering Object, \(P\) states the purpose,
\(M\) is the reconstruction and evaluation model, \(x\) contains the free
variables, \(q\) contains fixed inputs and context, \(\mathcal F\) is the
feasible set, \(J\) is the objective with units and scaling, and \(\Sigma\)
states the uncertainty boundary. None of these entries may be inferred from a
solver result.

The reader path now follows the dependencies of this contract:
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{engineering question}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{declared freedoms, fixed inputs, objective, constraints}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{numerical reconstruction and residual evaluation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{mathematical candidate and convergence evidence}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{applicability check and engineering evaluation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Engineering Decision outside the solver.}
\end{array}
\]

\section*{Why the technical chapters follow in this order}

Numerical reconstruction appears first because every trial curvature edit must
be turned into geometry before endpoint and observation residuals can be
evaluated. Alignment optimization then states the continuous and discrete
problem and introduces SQP, derivatives, and sparsity only when the growing
problem makes them necessary. The architecture chapter describes the
responsibility boundaries of a possible solver service and distinguishes them
from the limited experimental AXTRAN evidence. The final specialization asks
how a declared realization model may enter the evaluation without collapsing
model, realization, observation, or decision.

\begin{principle}[Optimization Principle]
\label{princ:optimization-principle}
An AIM optimization formulation shall declare its Engineering Object and
purpose, freedoms and fixed inputs, model, objective and scaling, constraints,
uncertainty boundary, and provenance. Its numerical output is a candidate with
residual and convergence evidence, not an Engineering Decision.
\end{principle}

\begin{summary}
Optimization does not turn evaluation into authority. It makes a declared
engineering question computationally searchable and returns candidates that
can be inspected, compared, and evaluated.

\[
\boxed{\text{calculated}\neq\text{applicable}\neq
\text{accepted}\neq\text{authoritative}}
\]
\end{summary}
