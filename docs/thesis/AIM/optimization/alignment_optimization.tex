\chapter{From an Alignment Problem to a Mathematical Candidate}

\section*{Reader bridge: the question carried into the mathematics}

The numerical evaluator can now reconstruct and test any permitted curvature
edit. The remaining task is to search those edits without forgetting the
engineering declaration that gave the numbers meaning.

For the CAD-curve/survey-polyline discrepancy, the chapter bundle uses the
following fixed reading contract:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.25\textwidth}p{0.65\textwidth}}
\toprule
Engineering Object & the identified Alignment; identity is preserved\\
Purpose & obtain candidates for reconciling a declared transition-region discrepancy\\
Free variables & selected intrinsic curvature parameters and only those explicitly released\\
Fixed inputs & start/end pose, locked elements, metric and observation context, source data\\
Constraints & continuity, fixed boundary conditions, declared bounds and applicability conditions\\
Objective & declared, unit-aware residual terms with recorded scaling and source\\
Output & candidate parameters, reconstructed representation, residuals, convergence and numerical evidence\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This contract is an illustrative formulation, not a universal AIM objective.

\section*{The route through the deep dive}

The following sections are cumulative. First, the engineering question is
written as a continuous curvature problem. Second, discretization exposes a
finite vector of freedoms and constraints. Third, SQP appears because the
reconstruction makes the problem nonlinear while equality and inequality
conditions must remain visible. Initial guesses matter because local methods
do not erase non-convexity. Finally, analytic derivatives, sparsity, and hybrid
parameterizations appear because they reduce computational work and make
influence traceable.

\paragraph{Reader bridge to discretization.}
The functional becomes computable only after a parameterization and grid

\paragraph{Reader bridge to SQP.}
Reconstruction couples curvature parameters nonlinearly to endpoint and
observation residuals. SQP is introduced as one local constrained method for
this visible need, not as the definition of optimization and not as evidence
of a general production solver.
\input{optimization/alignment/30_sqp_concrete}

\paragraph{Reader bridge to initialization.}
A converged local run answers a question near its starting point. Initial
guesses and multiple starts therefore belong to the provenance of a candidate
and cannot be hidden behind a success flag.
\input{optimization/alignment/40_initial_guesses}

\paragraph{Reader bridge to derivatives and sparsity.}
As the number of released curvature parameters grows, dense numerical
differentiation repeats expensive reconstruction and obscures local
dependency. Analytic structure and sparsity answer that cost and auditability
problem.

\paragraph{Reader bridge to alternative parameterizations.}
No single parameterization is best for every declared purpose. The final
section compares parametric and hybrid choices as model alternatives whose
freedoms and approximations must remain recorded.

\section*{What leaves the mathematical chapter}

The mathematical result is not merely \(x^\star\). A reviewable handover is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C=(\,&\mathcal P.id,M.id,x^\star,r(x^\star),J(x^\star),\\
             &\text{constraint margins},\text{termination},
             \varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}},\text{provenance}\,).
\end{aligned}
\]
Termination reports why the algorithm stopped; convergence evidence reports
what the numerical method established. Neither proves applicability to a
different context or acceptance by an engineer.

The next architecture chapter therefore asks how a service can preserve this
contract across ingestion, calculation, and review without silently committing
the candidate to the authoritative model.
