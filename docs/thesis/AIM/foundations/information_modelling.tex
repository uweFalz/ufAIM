\chapter{Information Modelling}

\label{chap:information-modelling}

% ============================================================
\section{Motivation}
% ============================================================

\begin{remark}
Information models describe, organize, exchange, and interpret
engineering knowledge.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Different engineering domains emphasize different aspects of reality.
Buildings, infrastructure systems, operational systems, and networks do
not all share the same modelling centre.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The present work introduces Alignment-based Information Modelling
(AIM) as a modelling paradigm centred on railway alignments and their
associated geometric, spatial, and operational information.
\end{remark}

% ============================================================
\section{BIM, AIM, and PIM}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Process Information Modelling]
\label{def:process-information-modelling}
In this thesis, the term Process Information Modelling, abbreviated as
PIM, is used as an umbrella concept for engineering information
modelling approaches that organize information not only around physical
assets, but also around processes, state evolution, lifecycle behaviour,
and operational context.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
PIM is not introduced here as an established standard.
It is used as a conceptual framing device for relating different
information modelling paradigms.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Building Information Modelling]
\label{def:building-information-modelling}
Building Information Modelling, abbreviated as BIM, denotes an
asset-oriented information modelling paradigm in which physical objects,
their properties, relations, and lifecycle information form the primary
organizing structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Alignment-based Information Modelling]
\label{def:alignment-based-information-modelling}
Alignment-based Information Modelling, abbreviated as AIM, denotes an
alignment-oriented information modelling paradigm in which railway
alignment, reference systems, admissible states, operational behaviour,
and realization processes form the primary organizing structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Within this thesis, BIM and AIM are interpreted as complementary
specializations within a broader process-oriented modelling perspective.
\end{remark}

\section{Why AIM?}
% ============================================================

\begin{remark}
Engineering information models are never neutral.

Every modelling paradigm implicitly chooses a central organizing
principle around which information is structured.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
In many traditional information models, physical assets constitute this
organizing principle.

Objects, components, assemblies, and their associated properties form
the primary modelling entities.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
This perspective has proven highly successful for buildings and many
other engineering disciplines.

However, railway systems exhibit characteristics that cannot be fully
explained through assets alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Railway operation is fundamentally governed by geometry.

Vehicle motion, cant, speed, comfort, safety, operational performance,
and infrastructure utilization all depend on the geometric structure of
the alignment.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The alignment is therefore not merely another infrastructure object.

Instead, it acts as the geometric framework within which railway
operation becomes possible.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Primary modelling entity]
\label{def:primary-modelling-entity}
Within AIM, the alignment is adopted as the primary modelling entity.

Other engineering information is interpreted relative to the alignment,
its reference systems, and its induced operational state space.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This choice does not imply that assets become unimportant.

Bridges, tunnels, switches, signals, stations, and other infrastructure
elements remain essential engineering objects.

However, within AIM they are interpreted as entities associated with,
located along, constrained by, or interacting with an alignment.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The resulting viewpoint shifts the modelling focus from static objects
toward geometry, motion, admissible states, and operational processes.
\end{remark}

% ============================================================
\section{Connection to AIM}
% ============================================================

\begin{summary}
BIM primarily organizes information around assets.

AIM primarily organizes information around alignments.

PIM is used in this thesis as a broader conceptual umbrella under which
asset-oriented, lifecycle-oriented, alignment-oriented, and
process-oriented modelling perspectives can coexist.

AIM therefore appears not as an isolated alternative to BIM, but as a
natural member of a larger family of engineering information modelling
approaches.
\end{summary}
