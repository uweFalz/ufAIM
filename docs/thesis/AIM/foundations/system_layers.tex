\chapter{Execution Order and Responsibility}
\label{chap:system-layers}

The preceding chapters already supply the concepts, state, operators,
and realization contract. The purpose of this chapter is only to show
their execution order and to identify where responsibility changes. It
does not introduce another architecture catalogue.

\section{The curvature-edit chain}

Starting from an identified alignment and an authorized curvature edit,
the formal machinery proceeds as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathrm{pose2}(s_0),\widetilde\kappa)
&\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal D\ }
\widetilde x_{\mathrm{intrinsic}}
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal G_c\ }
\widetilde x_{\mathrm{metric}}\\
&\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal P,\,\mathcal C\ }
\widetilde x_{\mathrm{comparable}}
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal E_c\ }
y_{\mathrm{qualified}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Reconstruction propagates the curvature edit. Metric realization makes
the state measurable. Spatial mappings and coordinate transformations
place qualified sources in a compatible comparison domain. Evaluation
produces the consequence. Each arrow has its own applicability and
provenance; no arrow changes alignment identity.

The chain uses the curvature relation
\cref{eq:curvature-relation}, reconstruction relation
\cref{eq:state-evolution}, and cant evolution relation
\cref{eq:cant-evolution}. Their execution precedes metric comparison;
they do not wait for a CAD representation to define the alignment.

\section{Where physical realization and observation enter}

Physical realization is not another step between intrinsic and metric
description. It relates conditions:
\[
x_{\mathrm{target}}
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal R\ }
x_{\mathrm{real}}.
\]
Observation supplies evidence about a condition through a measurement
process. Thus a survey polyline does not enter the main chain as
``reality.'' It enters as qualified evidence that may be mapped into a
compatible metric domain and compared with a qualified target or
realized state.

\section{Responsibility at each boundary}

The useful layer distinctions are consequences of the operator chain:

\begin{description}
\item[Intrinsic responsibility] owns the constructive controls,
  boundary conditions, and state propagation.
\item[Metric responsibility] owns the metric space, reference surface,
  embedding, and validity of measurable spatial consequences.
\item[Representation responsibility] owns sampling, coordinate form,
  precision, and exchange provenance.
\item[Physical responsibility] owns the relation between target and
  realized conditions.
\item[Observation responsibility] owns source, method, time,
  uncertainty, and evidential limitations.
\item[Evaluation responsibility] owns the comparison method,
  applicability, assumptions, and qualified result---not the decision.
\end{description}

\begin{principle}[Metric realization principle]
\label{princ:metric-realization-layer}
Semantic alignment logic is separated from metric realization behaviour.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Canonical separation principle]
\label{princ:canonical-layer-separation}
Intrinsic geometry, coordinate embedding, metric realization, physical
realization, observation, and evaluation shall not be merged into one
implicit operation.
\end{principle}

\section{Dependencies, not an implementation stack}

\begin{definition}[Canonical transformation structure]
\label{def:canonical-transformation-structure}
The formal transformation structure is an ordered composition of typed
operators whose intermediate results preserve identity, context, and
provenance.
\end{definition}

The ordering constrains meaning, not software deployment. Operations may
be cached, recomputed, or implemented by different tools provided their
contracts remain intact.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:alignment-not-crs-invariant}
A coordinate transformation may change metric properties of a
representation under projection; it does not change intrinsic alignment
identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:alignment-multi-layer-system}
An alignment consequence is trustworthy only when the constructive,
metric, representational, physical, observational, and evaluative
responsibilities on which it depends remain explicit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:operational-behaviour-emerges}
Operational consequences arise from declared compositions of state,
context, operators, and applicable engineering knowledge.
\end{proposition}

The result of the chain is therefore modest but useful: the theory can
say exactly what changed, which state followed, which action produced
each artifact, where it is measurable, which freedoms remain, and what
evidence may be compared. It still cannot turn a computed result into an
engineering decision.

The ellipsoid chapters now specialize this chain by supplying an
Earth-surface metric context.
