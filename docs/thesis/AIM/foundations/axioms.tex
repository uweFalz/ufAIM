\chapter{Axioms as Failure Boundaries}
\label{chap:axiomatic-foundation}

Conceptual Structure established why AIM must distinguish an engineering
object from its states, observations, evaluations, and representations.
The formal task is now to make those distinctions usable. Consider the
recurring case of a design alignment available as a CAD curve and a
surveyed condition available as a sampled polyline. The two geometries
can be compared computationally, but that comparison becomes meaningful
only after five common failure modes have been excluded.

\section{What the formal model must not collapse}

\begin{axiom}[Object continuity]
\label{ax:object-continuity}
An engineering object's identity is not replaced by a change of state,
observation, evaluation, or representation.
\end{axiom}

The CAD curve and survey polyline may concern one alignment, different
states of it, or even different objects. Geometric similarity cannot
settle that identity question; identity must already be established by
the engineering context in which the comparison is made.

\begin{axiom}[Construction--representation separation]
\label{ax:construction-representation-separation}
Constructive engineering knowledge and a representation derived from it
occupy different formal roles.
\end{axiom}

A sampled polyline can support rendering, exchange, or computation. Its
vertices do not thereby become the constructive definition of the
alignment, and resampling does not create a new authoritative object.

\begin{axiom}[Reality--observation separation]
\label{ax:reality-observation-separation}
A physical state and an observation of that state are distinct inputs to
engineering reasoning.
\end{axiom}

Survey points are source-grounded evidence. They do not constitute the
physical railway itself, and uncertainty or incompleteness in the
observation must not be transferred silently to physical reality.

\begin{axiom}[Evaluation--decision separation]
\label{ax:evaluation-decision-separation}
An evaluation produces a qualified result; it does not constitute an
engineering decision.
\end{axiom}

A computed deviation may support acceptance, maintenance planning, or
redesign. No threshold, residual, or solver output acquires decision
authority merely by being computed.

\begin{axiom}[Intrinsic--derived separation]
\label{ax:intrinsic-derived-separation}
Derived geometry does not replace the intrinsic construction from which
it is obtained.
\end{axiom}

Coordinates, tangents, offsets, and sampled vertices are useful
consequences. The formal construction must therefore expose the
dependencies that produce them.

\section{The constructive dependency}

Let \(I\subseteq\mathbb{R}\) be an interval parameterized by arc-length
\(s\). Let \(\kappa:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\) be curvature, and let
\(\theta:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\) and
\(\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}\) denote heading and planar
position. The mathematical axioms below state the dependency that turns
intrinsic construction into derived geometry.

\begin{axiom}[Arc-length parameterization]
\label{ax:arc-length-parameterization}
\[
\lVert\gamma'(s)\rVert=1
\qquad\text{for all }s\in I.
\]
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Heading evolution]
\label{ax:heading-evolution}
\[
\theta'(s)=\kappa(s).
\]
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Tangent reconstruction]
\label{ax:tangent-reconstruction}
\[
\gamma'(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta(s)\\
\sin\theta(s)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\end{axiom}

\begin{proposition}[Curvature-driven reconstruction]
\label{prop:curvature-driven-reconstruction}
Given an initial position \(\gamma(s_0)\), an initial heading
\(\theta(s_0)\), and an integrable curvature function
\(\kappa\in L^1(I)\), the planar trajectory is uniquely determined on
\(I\) as an absolutely continuous curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(\kappa\in L^1(I)\) both integrals below exist and define absolutely
continuous functions. Integration of \cref{ax:heading-evolution} gives
\[
\theta(s)=\theta(s_0)+\int_{s_0}^{s}\kappa(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma.
\]
Substitution into \cref{ax:tangent-reconstruction} and a second
integration give
\[
\gamma(s)=\gamma(s_0)+
\int_{s_0}^{s}
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta(\sigma)\\
\sin\theta(\sigma)
\end{pmatrix}
\dd\sigma.
\]
The initial conditions fix the remaining integration constants.
\end{proof}

This result is deliberately limited. It reconstructs a planar trajectory
from stated inputs and assumptions. It does not establish object
identity, prove that a survey observes the same state, confer authority
on a representation, or decide whether a discrepancy is acceptable.
