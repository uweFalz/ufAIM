\chapter*{Mathematical Foundations}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Mathematical Foundations}

\begin{remark}
The preceding chapters introduced the motivation for AIM and argued that
railway engineering cannot be fully understood through traditional
object-oriented descriptions alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
However, motivation alone is insufficient.

Before alignments can be represented, analysed, realized, or optimized,
a rigorous mathematical foundation must be established.
\end{remark}

\section*{What do we already know?}

\begin{remark}
Railway systems possess an intrinsic geometric structure.

Vehicle motion, cant, stationing, operational behaviour, and
engineering constraints all depend on this structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The previous part identified the need for a modelling framework centred
on railway alignments rather than solely on engineering assets.
\end{remark}

\section*{What is still missing?}

\begin{remark}
A modelling framework requires a precise mathematical language.

At this point, concepts such as alignment, geometry, state, and
reference systems have been discussed only on an intuitive level.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
AIM therefore requires a formal foundation capable of describing railway
geometry independently of implementation details, software systems, or
engineering conventions.
\end{remark}

\section*{What comes next?}

\begin{remark}
This part develops the mathematical basis of AIM.

Particular emphasis is placed on curvature-driven geometry,
arc-length-based descriptions, state representations, and the operators
that connect geometric, physical, and operational interpretations.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
These concepts form the foundation upon which all later discussions of
runtime behaviour, realization, optimization, and information modelling
are built.
\end{remark}

\section*{Relation to the AIM Roadmap}

\begin{remark}
Within the AIM roadmap (see Figure~\ref{fig:aim-roadmap}),
the present part corresponds primarily to the transition from classical
alignment engineering toward curvature space, intrinsic geometry, and
the first state descriptions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The objective is not yet to describe complete railway systems, but to
establish the mathematical building blocks from which such systems can
later be constructed.
\end{remark}
