\chapter{Dynamic Balance as a Qualified Residual}
\label{chap:dynamic-balance}

\section*{One calculation, six different roles}

Consider the reduced point-following test \(M_{\mathrm{red}}\) in a local
track frame \(F_{\mathrm{track}}\). Positive curvature and positive lateral
action use the same declared side; positive cant angle \(\phi\) compensates
that action. The test assumes rigid traversal, prescribed \(v(t)\), small
local extent and a uniform gravity magnitude \(g\). It omits vehicle mass and
inertia, suspension, wheel--rail contact, track flexibility, irregularities,
aerodynamics and control.

\begin{principle}[Dynamic-balance principle]
\label{princ:dynamic-balance}
Dynamic balance is a model-, frame-, sign- and purpose-qualified relation.
\end{principle}

\section{Balance relation}

\begin{definition}[Balance relation]
\label{def:balance-relation}
For \(M_{\mathrm{red}}\), the balance residual is
\[
r_{\mathrm{bal}}(t)
=v(t)^2\kappa(s(t))-g\sin\phi(s(t)).
\]
The reduced balance condition is \(r_{\mathrm{bal}}=0\).
\end{definition}

\[
\boxed{\text{zero reduced balance residual}
\not\Rightarrow \text{zero vehicle-body acceleration}}
\]

Equality cancels two terms in this one frame and model. It does not establish
multibody equilibrium, predicted vehicle response, measured response,
passenger perception, safety or comfort.

\begin{definition}[Effective lateral acceleration]
\label{def:dynamics-effective-lateral-acceleration}
The historical notation \(a_{\mathrm{eff}}\) denotes
\(r_{\mathrm{bal}}\) in this reduced test. Calling it an acceleration does
not expand its evidential meaning.
\end{definition}

\section{Cant deficiency, excess and trajectory}

\begin{definition}[Cant deficiency]
\label{def:cant-deficiency}
Within the declared signs and \(M_{\mathrm{red}}\), \(r_{\mathrm{bal}}>0\)
is reduced cant deficiency. A standard-specific quantity still requires its
own definition and applicability.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Excess cant]
\label{def:excess-cant}
Within the same qualifications, \(r_{\mathrm{bal}}<0\) is reduced excess cant.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Balance trajectory]
\label{def:dynamics-balance-trajectory}
The balance trajectory is \(r_{\mathrm{bal}}(t)\), or
\(r_{\mathrm{bal}}(s)\) only where velocity has been supplied as a function of
station.
\end{definition}

For variable speed,
\[
\dot r_{\mathrm{bal}}
=2v\dot v\kappa+v^3\kappa'-gv\cos\phi\,\phi',
\]
under the same assumptions. The derivative adds exposure rate; it still adds
no vehicle response.

\[
\boxed{\text{predicted response}
\not\Rightarrow \text{acceptable engineering state}}
\]

The CAD curve and survey polyline make the separation concrete. Either may be
realized into a comparable track-frame curvature estimate, but a deviation
between them is not automatically the cause of a measured body acceleration.
That causal claim requires an identified response model, matched observations
and qualified evidence. The following Vienna example is read with exactly
