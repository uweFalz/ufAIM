\chapter{Operational Velocity}

\label{chap:operational-velocity}

\section*{Motivation}

Railway alignment geometry is naturally parameterized by arc length,
whereas operational behaviour is experienced through time.

Operational velocity is therefore the bridge between intrinsic geometry
and runtime dynamics.

\begin{principle}[Velocity-runtime principle]
\label{princ:velocity-runtime}
Within AIM, velocity is interpreted as a runtime quantity coupling
arc-length-based railway geometry to time-based operational behaviour.
\end{principle}

\section{Arc Length and Time}

\begin{definition}[Velocity relation]
\label{def:velocity-relation}
For an operational velocity \(v(s)\), arc-length and time derivatives
are related by:
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}
=
v
\frac{\dd}{\dd s}.
\]
\end{definition}

This relation is fundamental for translating curvature evolution into
operational runtime effects.

\section{Design Velocity and Operational Velocity}

\begin{definition}[Design velocity]
\label{def:design-velocity}
Design velocity denotes the velocity assumption used during alignment
design.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Operational velocity]
\label{def:operational-velocity}
Operational velocity denotes the realized or intended runtime velocity
during operation.
\end{definition}

Design velocity and operational velocity need not be identical.

\section{Velocity Envelopes}

\begin{definition}[Velocity envelope]
\label{def:velocity-envelope}
A velocity envelope defines admissible velocity ranges along an
alignment.
\end{definition}

Velocity envelopes interact with curvature, cant, comfort,
admissibility, and operational constraints.

\section{Connection to AIM}

\begin{summary}
Operational velocity connects arc-length-based geometry with
time-dependent runtime behaviour.

Within AIM, velocity is not treated as an external scalar only, but as a
runtime coupling quantity connecting curvature, cant, dynamics,
admissibility, and optimization.
\end{summary}
