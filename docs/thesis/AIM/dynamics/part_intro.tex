The identified straight--transition--arc Alignment does not stop at the Part V
boundary. At each intrinsic position \(s\), its curvature law, realized pose,
profile, cant law, and qualified moving frame already determine the railway
state needed here. Part VI therefore introduces no second state model. It asks
one new question:

\begin{quote}
How does traversal through this spatial state generate time-dependent
quantities, and which additional models are required before an engineering
consequence can be evaluated?
\end{quote}

The new input is a traversal law
\[
s=s(t),\qquad v(t)=\frac{\dd s}{\dd t}.
\]
Composition turns spatial laws into temporal exposure:
\(\kappa(s)\mapsto\kappa(s(t))\) and \(u(s)\mapsto u(s(t))\).
Neither law changes identity. What changes is the rate at which a vehicle or
other moving system encounters them.

Every arrow in \cref{fig:space-to-time-dependency} adds information:
the traversal law adds time; its derivatives add velocity and acceleration;
a reduced relation adds stated kinematic assumptions; a response model adds
vehicle and track physics; evaluation adds purpose and applicable rules; only
an Engineering Decision adds authorized commitment.

\begin{principle}[Dynamic-State Interpretation Principle]
\label{princ:dynamic-state-interpretation}
Time-dependent quantities are derived by composing the qualified railway state
with a qualified traversal law and, where required, explicit response models.
Reduced kinematics, response, evaluation, and decision remain distinct.
\end{principle}

\begin{summary}
Geometry supplies the spatial law. Traversal supplies time. Models supply
response. Purpose and rules supply evaluation. Authority supplies decision.
\end{summary}
