\begin{remark}
This part places the AIM framework into the context of railway
engineering practice.
Mathematical models alone are insufficient unless they can be related to
engineering constraints, standards, and operational rules.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The purpose of this part is therefore to connect formal modelling with
practical admissibility conditions arising from railway design and
operation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The following chapters discuss standards, rule systems, and structured
constraint formulations relevant to alignment engineering.
\end{remark}
